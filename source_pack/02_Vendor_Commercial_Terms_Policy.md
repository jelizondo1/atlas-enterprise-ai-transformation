# MERIDIAN RETAIL GROUP
## Vendor Commercial Terms & Accounts Payable Dispute Policy

**Document ID:** PROC-AP-208  
**Version:** 5.1  
**Owner:** Procurement Operations / Accounts Payable  
**Effective date:** March 1, 2026

### 1. Document precedence
Unless the applicable contract states otherwise:
1. Executed contract amendment
2. Executed promotional or commercial addendum
3. Master Supplier Agreement
4. Purchase order
5. Approved change order
6. Supplier invoice

Informal email correspondence does not ordinarily override executed commercial documentation. If an email appears to authorize a material exception not reflected in an executed agreement, Procurement must confirm validity before payment.

### 2. Price discrepancies
Invoice unit prices should agree with the active PO price or an applicable executed agreement that supersedes it.

A price variance may be accepted without Procurement involvement when the total disputed amount is below $250, the variance is below 1% of the applicable PO line value, and there is no recurring issue.

The tolerance does not apply to promotional pricing, rebates, regulated items, contractually fixed rates, or intentional surcharges.

### 3. Promotional and temporary pricing
Promotional pricing is valid only when supplier, SKU/product family, effective date, geography, and agreed price/discount mechanism match the executed promotional agreement.

An email stating that a promotion was extended is not sufficient by itself unless the sender had documented commercial authority and Procurement confirms the extension.

### 4. Quantity discrepancies
For physical goods, payment should generally be based on quantity accepted into Meridian inventory. Supplier proof of delivery alone does not override documented shortages or damage.

If receiving records are missing but credible proof of delivery exists, refer to the receiving location for confirmation.

### 5. Missing goods receipts
Merchandise invoices normally require a valid goods receipt. An exception may be made when goods were received but the receipt was not posted due to documented system/operational error and Receiving or an authorized warehouse manager confirms receipt.

### 6. Freight charges
Standard freight structures:
- **PREPAID_INCLUDED:** supplier arranges transportation; freight included in product price; separate freight charges should not be paid.
- **PREPAID_ADD:** supplier arranges transportation and may separately invoice approved freight with support.
- **COLLECT:** Meridian arranges and pays the carrier; supplier invoices should not ordinarily include freight.

Executed supplier agreements override PO freight terms unless a later approved amendment exists.

### 7. Duplicate invoices
A probable duplicate normally matches supplier, invoice number, invoice amount, and legal entity. Matching amount alone is insufficient. If an invoice number was legitimately reused, verify underlying goods/services before rejecting.

### 8. Tax discrepancies
AP may correct clerical tax discrepancies only when treatment is determinable from Meridian's approved tax configuration. Uncertain jurisdiction, exemption, withholding, unusual cross-border treatment, or supplier disagreement must be referred to Tax.

### 9. Credit notes
A credit note or corrected invoice is generally required for supplier-originated overbilling, incorrect supplier-generated charges, or formal tax correction. It is not required when Meridian's own PO/receiving/internal processing caused the discrepancy and the supplier invoice was correct.

### 10. Contract-related disputes
AP may apply clear contractual terms. Refer to Procurement when documents conflict, the applicable amendment cannot be identified, language reasonably supports more than one interpretation, the supplier challenges the interpretation, or the decision may establish a material precedent.

### 11. Supplier dispute window
Suppliers should raise payment disputes within **90 calendar days** unless the contract provides a different period, Meridian caused delay, or Procurement/Finance approves older review.

### 12. Strategic and priority suppliers
Priority affects case prioritization, service-level expectations, and escalation visibility. It does not by itself change payment authority, evidence requirements, contract interpretation, or approval thresholds.

### 13. Partial adjustments
The record must state total disputed amount, amount accepted, amount rejected, and calculation basis. Approval authority is based on the original disputed amount.

### 14. Historical treatment
Prior cases may provide context but do not automatically establish the correct outcome. Previous cases may use outdated terms, contain errors, reflect one-time exceptions, or have been approved by someone with special authority.

### 15. Evidence quality
Primary evidence includes executed contracts/amendments, POs, goods receipts, authorized receiving confirmation, transportation records, formal credit notes, and approved pricing agreements.

Supporting evidence includes emails, screenshots, prior case notes, and informal spreadsheets. Supporting evidence should not override contradictory primary evidence without appropriate review.
